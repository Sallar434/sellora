Day 1: everything is set up. Month 1: traffic

For every seller. On the day they sign up, Autopilot builds the store, lists every product, sets up SEO, AEO, GEO and the first blogs, schedules 30 days of posts and reels, drafts the ads and switches on WhatsApp. Then this 30-day sprint runs. Autopilot checks it every week and changes course if a checkpoint is missed.

Every visitor is sent to the website. Every bio link, post, reel, story, pin, blog post, ad and WhatsApp message links straight to the product page on the store, with tracking tags so Autopilot knows which post or ad brought each visit and sale. WhatsApp is used to follow up on carts, confirm orders and answer questions, not instead of the website.

Where website visits come from in month 1: daily posts and reels linking to products, Google free listings, the starter blog posts, sharing on WhatsApp and, if the seller set a budget, ads sent to the website. SEO, AEO and GEO start on day 1, but Google and AI search usually take 2–6 months to rank a new site, so search traffic grows later. Google also needs a few days to review a Merchant Center feed and to index new pages.

Days 1–3: live and shared

Store live with COD. Every product listed and on Google free listings. A share message and QR code for the store link go to the seller’s WhatsApp contacts and Status. Every social bio links to the store. Checkpoint: store live, first website visitors.

Main number every week: website visitors, then the share who add to cart and buy.

Days 4–10: be seen

One post and one reel every day on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, each linking to a product page. Product tags in posts open the store. Blog posts go out to bring search visitors. A launch discount for the first 20 buyers. Checkpoint: people adding to cart.

Days 11–20: convert

WhatsApp cart reminders and COD confirmation switch on. Autopilot fixes the page where most visitors leave. If the seller set a budget, a small test ad sends people to the best product’s page on the website. Checkpoint: first order.

Days 21–30: repeat what works

More posts and budget go to the product and post that sold. Buyers are asked for a review and a referral. The day-30 report shows what worked. Checkpoint: second and third orders.

First-sale promise (suggested offer)

If a seller completes the setup checklist and has no order in 30 days, VendOm refunds that month’s plan. Autopilot can’t guarantee sales, but VendOm can guarantee the seller doesn’t pay for a month that brought none. It also shows we trust the product.

What Autopilot does each month, by plan

Every plan gets day-1 setup, the 30-day sprint, SEO, AEO, GEO and the WhatsApp agent. Bigger plans get more posts, reels, blogs, channels and ad management. Monthly amounts reset each month, and unused amounts don’t carry over.

One post is published on every connected channel, rewritten for each, and counts once. Costs assume every allowance is fully used. Most sellers use 30–50%, so real profit is higher. Profit is after AI, payment fees and hosting. Images average $0.018: a cheap model for social graphics, a premium model for product photos. Video clips use a fast video model at about $0.10 a second, with one retry allowed. The ad budget is paid by the seller to Meta, Google and TikTok, on top of the plan. “Cap” is the most ad spend Autopilot will manage on that plan.

Brand Builder: an optional 6-month plan for new brands

For sellers starting a brand with its own name, look and voice, not for resellers or stores that only want sales. The seller chooses it at sign-up or later. It is the same programme on every plan (Grow or higher recommended, because it uses TikTok and Google ads), because the spend is large and needs one proven method. Day-1 setup and the 30-day sprint still run. Brand Builder adds a brand identity, a content style and a planned ad budget that grows month by month.
